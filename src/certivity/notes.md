Notes on this regulation corpus (UN Regulations under the 1958 Agreement). Replace this file, or point
CERTIVITY_NOTES at another one, when working with a different set of obligations.

- Many regulations approve a vehicle type for a system (steering, braking, occupant protection). A component
  supplier must still meet the paragraphs that set requirements on its part, how it is marked, how it is tested,
  and how it is fitted to the vehicle. Paragraphs about other parts of that system, and about the approval
  authority's own procedure, are not the component's requirements.
- Some regulations approve a device (a lamp, a retro-reflector, a seat belt); others regulate how devices are
  installed on the vehicle. A device usually needs both: its own device regulation and the installation
  regulation that covers it.
- Lighting: the list holds both the older single-device lighting regulations and the consolidated ones that
  replaced them. For a new product cite only the consolidated regulation, never the one it replaced:
  UN R148 (light-signalling devices) replaces UN R4, R6, R7, R23, R38, R50, R77, R87 and R91;
  UN R149 (road illumination devices) replaces UN R19, R98, R112, R113, R119 and R123;
  UN R150 (retro-reflective devices) replaces UN R3, R27, R69, R70 and R104.
- A UN Global Technical Regulation (GTR) and the UN Regulation that transposes it are separate entries in the
  list; cite each one whose scope covers the product.
- Electronics (control units, LEDs, sensors, switches, heaters) bring in UN R10 electromagnetic compatibility.
  Software update or cyber-security regulations (UN R155, UN R156) concern the vehicle manufacturer's
  management systems, not a component's own design.
- Annexes often hold the test procedures and pass criteria that the main text refers to. Keep the ones that test
  this product; skip forms, communication templates, and examples of approval marks for other devices.
